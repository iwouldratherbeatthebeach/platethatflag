About Flag That Plate

An independent reference for the diplomatic license plates issued in the United States, and a log of the ones people spot.

What it is

Flag That Plate was created out of interest in diplomatic license plates and the countries and mission roles they represent. It is an independent reference and observation project intended for educational and informational use. It is not affiliated with the U.S. Department of State.

The site logs lookup activity to improve the plate database over time. Unknown codes may be reviewed and added as new plate assignments are observed.

How the codes work

Diplomatic plates in the United States are issued by the State Department's Office of Foreign Missions. Each country or organization has a two-letter code, and the plate list here has 213 of them. A letter before the code gives the holder's role, a D after it marks a diplomat at the United Nations, and the numbers are the plate's serial.

How plates for Japan, country code AF, are read

DAF 0789D, before the codeAFA diplomat from Japan
CAF 0789C, before the codeAFA consul from Japan
SAF 0789S, before the codeAFEmbassy or mission staff from Japan
AFD 0789D, after the codeAFA Japanese diplomat at the United Nations

A few codes are listed for two missions. QX, for example, appears for both Pakistan and Iran (DC only): Iran's interests in Washington are represented through Pakistan's embassy. The decoder names both when that happens.

What we record

When you look up a plate, the site records the country, the plate you typed and the time, together with a one-way hash of your IP address, your browser's user agent, the country your connection comes from, and your username if you're signed in. Codes it doesn't recognize are logged for review, and a vehicle sighting stores the details you enter. These events may also be sent to the site's operational monitoring.
